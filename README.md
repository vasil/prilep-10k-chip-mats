# Chip Mats on the Route

The 10 km of **Че Трчаме!**, Prilep, 18 October 2026, as recorded from a
wheelchair — and the race's own timing mats laid over it.

The ten official splits are placed on the GPS track by the **clock time** of
each one rather than by distance, which is what makes the two measurements
comparable: where the official mat says 7.5 km, the recording says 7.64.

It turns out there were only **four physical mats**. The course is a loop and
they were crossed ten times between them.

## Using it

| Key | |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Space</kbd> | play the race back — the dot moves at the recorded pace |
| <kbd>1</kbd> <kbd>2</kbd> <kbd>3</kbd> <kbd>4</kbd> | ×1, ×10, ×60, ×300 |
| <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> | move along the route, 10 m (Shift 100 m) |
| <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> | move the selected mat, 5 m (Shift 50 m) |
| <kbd>j</kbd> <kbd>k</kbd> | jump to the next / previous mat |
| <kbd>C</kbd> / <kbd>D</kbd> | place all mats by the official clock / by our own distance |
| <kbd>0</kbd> / <kbd>R</kbd> | put one back / reset all |
| <kbd>F</kbd> / <kbd>Enter</kbd> | centre / zoom in |

The replay follows recorded time, not distance, so it stands still where the
rider did.

## Where the numbers come from

- **Official splits** — TagTiming, bib 137. Chip 1:41:59, gun 1:42:51.
- **The track** — a phone recording at 1 Hz GPS alongside 120 Hz inertial
  sensors, part of the [WayTrace](https://github.com/) road-quality work for
  the Open Streets Initiative.

Our distance reads about 2.2% long against the measured course, growing
steadily from 30 m at the start to 220 m at the finish — what a
single-frequency phone does over 10 km, where every wobble adds length and
none of it subtracts.
